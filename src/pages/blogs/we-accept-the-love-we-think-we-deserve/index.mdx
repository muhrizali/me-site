A few months back when I was looking for a movie to watch, I came across a video on YouTube recommending some good Hollywood movies. One of the movies in that list was "The Perks Of Being A Wallflower". Few months after that (didn't saw the movie) a book YouTuber recommended the book that this movie was based upon.

_Man, you sure spend a lot of time on "best books" or "best movies" lists than on consuming actual books or movies._

Yeah you are right about that. This is a thing that I do. I kind of like it, like being mindful of what I am going to watch or read instead of mindlessly picking something. Sometimes I do like mindlessly watching something but nowadays I think all of us are moving away from that era of cable TVs. Oh man, you have really distracted me from my point. Just get the hell out of here.

Thinking now that I have watched the title so many times I ought to read the original book. Also a simple search on the internet revealed that the book was one of the most wildly popular books of all time. I need to get on the train quick.

And so I did read the book on my Kindle which I got few weeks after the book recommendation. The book was about a seemingly-awkward boy named Charlie moving to high-school, moving through his adolescent life, making friends and learning about love. The book was about growing up.

Before reading the book, I thought the story was similar to other western media written for teenagers or young adults where the characters just make a bigger deal out of the situations than they need to. I thought it was just about any other movie or drama where the characters just meaninglessly suffer from depression, trauma, anxiety (or some other buzz words) and about their stupid ways to deal with it.

I have liked the book after I have finished it but not that much. I just thought it was an okay book. But there was something about it that left me thinking. It gave me a lot of things to think about in my own life and the people around me. There are some books that leave some impression in your mind, and this was one of them.

Most of all I kept thinking about that one sentence. The one sentence that might have conviced me the book was not a bad one. 

> "We accept the love we think we deserve"
> -- Steve Chbowsky

Months after reading the book, I think I may have slowly starting to get the meaning and reality behind this quote.

## BOTTOM OF THE HEART

I was sitting with a friend named "Leaf" on a bench, enjoying the peace and serenity of the greenery around us. The slightly cold wind was swaying slowly through our hair and ears, and it was soothing. I hadn't went outside for a few months and this felt very nice. 

It was when we shift the topic to a mutual friend named "Moon". Now Leaf had told me before that she knew Moon and was friends with her. I was curious about it because both of the girls personalities were very different.

"How did you know Moon? Where did you meet her?" I asked her.

"Through Pot. We met a few times when she hangs out with Pot" she replied. "Pot" was another friend we both knew.

I was somewhat getting confused by that because I couldn't imagine Moon hanging out with Pot. I knew Pot and Moon quite well and she has made it certain that she didn't like Pot that much.

"You do know that Moon and Pot are in a relationship, don't you?" Leaf added after a brief pause.

"Moon and Pot? In a relationship? Are you serious?" I asked almost immediately.

"Yeah really. They have been together for a long time and I'd say they are pretty serious too" she replied.

"Serious? You mean are they... you know like... intimate with each other?" I asked, when I simply cannot picture them together and getting "intimate".

"Yeah, I think they are... they must be" she added. On the outside, I appeared as if we were having a pretty normal conversation about our friends. But there was something strange brewing inside me, there was something uncomfortable in me that I couldn't sit still. I tried hard to make myself calm and at peace with what was going on.

Moon was a girl that I have liked for a long time. I really liked her company when we used to go out and thought this was one thing I didn't know I longed for all my life (a story for another time). I tried getting to know her for almost 2-3 years, when Leaf dropped this bomb on me.

It does not matter how much I cry over this or how much I convince myself that it was all my fault (liking her in the first place, looking for obvious signs that she is not into me or not changing myself into someone who she'd like), it was ultimately never about me. It was always about her, and I never had (shouldn't have) control over that.

## LATE-NIGHT-NO-SLEEP THOUGHTS

When allowed the freedom, people always accept the love they think they deserve. Unlike us our mind knows our self-worth better than us. It weighs the attributes, features and personality of a prospective partner for us and makes the decision almost unconsciously. 

It can really go one of two ways; 
- the mind decides that the guy's/girl's values are way below or way above than us and its a reject
- or the mind can decide that the person values perfectly matches our self-worth, in which case its a clear acceptance

This unconscious decision can be made at the first impression of a person ("love at first sight") or after some period of time getting to know him or her. The most important point however, is not the result of the decision our mind makes but in realizing that it can be totally (sometimes severely) wrong any time.

There is also the matter of "acting" or "pretending" to be someone. People get attracted to partners who pretend to be someone, acting as if that is their real-self. The unconscious decision of acceptance made by the mind does not account whether the person of interest is really the one they claim to be or not. When one partner realizes the other one is fake, the attraction diminishes and the relationship crashes down.

Have you ever seen how some couples seems unmatched for one another? How they just dont't look right for each other? The love interest that a person chooses for oneself (casually or seriously) tells us more about how that person sees himself or herself. I think that tells us how much that person respects himself or herself and how clearly he/she sees their self-worth.

The same thing must have happened in Moon's case. She must have seen herself deserving Pot more than me. Unconsciously she must have seen herself wanting to spend the future with Pot more appropriate, than me.

As for me, it must have worked the same way. My mind would also have nudged me into her direction thinking that she was a fine one to spend the life with. Only to realize later down the road that she was not the one she claimed (or pretended) to be, that she was never real with me.
